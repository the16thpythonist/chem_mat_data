    ChEMBL and ShareAlike — how ChemMatData answered it

    Status: DECIDED, 2026-08-19, by the maintainer — option A, comply. The twelve ChEMBL-derived datasets are labelled CC-BY-SA-3.0 via a new license metadata field. See ADR 009. Attribution (§4 step 1) was completed 2026-08-18; labelling (§4 steps 2 and 4) on 2026-08-19. This file is kept as the evidence behind that decision. Written 2026-08-18. Not legal advice — this is research assembled so that a person can decide, with the evidence in one place instead of being re-derived every time.

    Why this file exists

    The dataset-extraction run esnuel_mca (Ree et al., Chem. Sci. 2025, 10.1039/D4SC07297A) blocked at its licence gate on two grounds. The first is specific to that paper and is dealt with in ../.dataset_work/esnuel_mca/reviews/license.md.

    The second is not specific to it at all: the molecules come from ChEMBL, and ChEMBL is CC BY-SA 3.0 — ShareAlike. The extraction skill's policy escalates ShareAlike to a human, correctly. But answering it for this one dataset would leave the same question to be re-answered on the next ChEMBL-derived paper, and there will be many: ChEMBL underlies a large share of med-chem ML data.

    And the question is not hypothetical or forward-looking. ChemMatData already redistributes eight ChEMBL-derived datasets, none of which attributes ChEMBL. That was discovered while investigating this run, not by design.

    So the decision to take is a project policy, and it has a retrospective half as well as a prospective one.

    1. What ChEMBL's licence actually says

    Verified against three independent current sources — this is not inferred from context.

    ChEMBL interface documentation, "Data Licensing" (https://chembl.gitbook.io/chembl-interface-documentation/about):

    "Access to the web interface of ChEMBL is made under the EBI's Terms of Use. The ChEMBL data is made available on a Creative Commons Attribution-Share Alike 3.0 Unported License."

    ChEMBL FAQ — this is the one that spells out propagation (https://chembl.gitbook.io/chembl-interface-documentation/frequently-asked-questions/general-questions):

    "The ChEMBL database is licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution-Share Alike 3.0 Unported License. This allows use, redistribution and adaption of ChEMBL as long as appropriate attribution is given (e.g., cite the current ChEMBL paper) and ensure that any adaptations are redistributed under the same license."

    The current release's own files — https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/chembl/ChEMBLdb/latest/README:

    "The data content in ChEMBL is licensed under a highly permissive Creative Commons license - specifically the "CC Attribution-ShareAlike 3.0 Unported license", see LICENSE file. The required attribution should contain the url of the ChEMBL resource, and also the release version, e.g.:

    ChEMBL data is from http://www.ebi.ac.uk/chembl - the version of ChEMBL is chembl_37.

    This should be visible on the entry portal for a web resource in which ChEMBL is integrated, or contained with the documentation for any further distribution."

    No change of terms was found. Consistent from the 2009 licensing FAQ through chembl_37 today. No migration to CC-BY or CC0.

    One near-miss, recorded so nobody trips on it. An August 2025 ChEMBL blog post releases a text-mined IUPAC-name file "under a CC0 license". That is SureChEMBL, a different resource and a single auxiliary file. It is not ChEMBL core and does not change anything here.

    EMBL-EBI's umbrella terms neither add nor remove anything — they place "no additional restrictions … other than those provided by the original data owners", and resource-specific terms "prevail" (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/about/terms-of-use/, rev. 2024-02-05).

    Three things ShareAlike here is not

    Worth stating plainly, because ShareAlike gets pattern-matched to NonCommercial and they are not the same animal:

    • It is not NonCommercial. ChEMBL has been explicit since 2012 that the SA condition does not preclude commercial use. Nothing here stops a company training on the data.
    • It is not a prohibition on redistribution. ChEMBL's own FAQ says the licence "allows use, redistribution and adaption". Redistribution is invited, on conditions.
    • It is not the CSD situation. The tmQMg-L / CSD entry in rejected_datasets.md rests on a rightsholder whose published position is that redistribution requires a signed agreement. ChEMBL's position is the opposite. These two cases should not be reasoned about together.

    2. What ShareAlike actually obliges — read from the licence text

    The load-bearing distinction is Adaptation vs Collection, and CC BY-SA 3.0 Unported defines both (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/legalcode.en):

    Adaptation means a work based upon the Work … including in any form recognizably derived from the original, except that a work that constitutes a Collection will not be considered an Adaptation for the purpose of this License.

    Collection means a collection of literary or artistic works … in which the Work is included in its entirety in unmodified form along with one or more other contributions, each constituting separate and independent works in themselves, which together are assembled into a collective whole.

    §4(b), the ShareAlike clause, ends with the sentence that bounds how far the obligation travels:

    "This Section 4(b) applies to the Adaptation as incorporated in a Collection, but this does not require the Collection apart from the Adaptation itself to be made subject to the terms of the Applicable License."

    §4(a) says the same thing about the unmodified Work.

    What that means concretely for this project. ShareAlike would attach to the individual ChEMBL-derived dataset. It would not reach:

    • the ChemMatData catalogue as a whole,
    • the other 38 datasets in it,
    • the chem_mat_data package source code.

    So the worst case is per-dataset licensing, not a copyleft event that swallows the project. That is a materially smaller problem than "ShareAlike" sounds like at first reading, and it is the single most useful fact in this document.

    The genuine open questions in the licence text

    Three, and they are why this is a judgement call rather than a lookup:

    1. Is a processed ChemMatData dataset an "Adaptation" or a "Collection"? A Collection requires the Work "in its entirety in unmodified form". A ChemMatData dataset is a filtered subset re-encoded as graphs — closer to an Adaptation. But the boundary is not crisp for data, and CC's definitions are written for literary and artistic works.

    2. Are the individual data points even protected? A SMILES string and a measured Ki are facts. Facts are not copyrightable. What copyright can subsist in is the selection and arrangement of the database. Whether extracting 50,000 structures takes a protected "substantial part" of that selection is exactly the kind of question that has no clean answer without a lawyer.

    3. CC BY-SA 3.0 Unported does not mention database rights at all. Checked directly against the legal code: zero occurrences of "database" and zero of "sui generis". The §3 grant is a copyright grant, and the "Work" definition reaches "a compilation of data to the extent it is protected as a copyrightable work". The EU/UK sui generis database right is a separate right that 3.0 Unported does not expressly license — CC only addressed this properly in the ported EU 3.0 licences and in 4.0. EMBL-EBI sits in that jurisdiction. This cuts both ways: the licence may not clearly grant database rights, and equally may not clearly impose ShareAlike on extractions that engage them.

    None of these is resolvable by reading harder. They are why this needs a person.

    4. What compliance would concretely require

    If the decision is to comply rather than to conclude SA does not attach, the work is small and mostly mechanical:

    1. Attribution — DONE 2026-08-18. Added to each affected dataset's sources and a notes entry: ChEMBL data is from http://www.ebi.ac.uk/chembl - the version of ChEMBL is chembl_NN, plus the ChEMBL paper (Mendez et al., Nucleic Acids Res. 2019, 47, D930–D940, 10.1093/nar/gky1075). Where the release is unknown, say so explicitly rather than omitting the line.
    2. A per-dataset licence field. metadata.yml has no licence key today. Adding one is independently worth doing — it is the natural place for this, and for the CC-BY attributions the extraction pipeline already collects and then drops.
    3. Mark that changes were made. §3(b) requires an Adaptation to "take reasonable steps to clearly label, demarcate or otherwise identify that changes were made". The descriptions largely do this already by describing the processing.
    4. State CC-BY-SA-3.0 on the ChEMBL-derived datasets if they are treated as Adaptations.
    5. Decide what this means for cmdata's users — arguably nothing more than that the dataset they downloaded carries a licence they can read.

    Steps 1–3 are worth doing under any of the options below, including "SA does not attach": attribution is a scientific-credit obligation before it is a legal one, and ChEMBL asks for it in a specific form.

    What was actually done on 2026-08-18

    Step 1 only. Steps 2, 4 and 5 are untouched, because they encode a licence decision that has not been taken — nothing here labels any dataset CC-BY-SA-3.0.

    • https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chembl/ added to sources for all eight datasets.
    • A notes entry added to all eight, naming ChEMBL as the source of the structures, stating that ChEMBL is licensed CC BY-SA 3.0, and citing Mendez et al.

    The shipped credit is deliberately generic and names no release version — a maintainer decision on 2026-08-18, taken for uniformity across the eight. Note that this is technically incomplete against ChEMBL's own formula, which asks that the attribution "contain the url of the ChEMBL resource, and also the release version". The versions that were established are not lost: they are recorded in the exposure table above, and can be folded into the shipped notes at any time if the project later decides to comply to the letter. - The same text was written into both chem_mat_data/metadata.yml (so it is live now) and each dataset's METADATA dict in chem_mat_data/scripts/ (so that re-running cmmanage metadata collect regenerates it rather than dropping it). The two were then checked to be byte-identical. - create_graph_datasets__dpp4.py had no METADATA dict at all — its catalogue entry is hand-curated and a regeneration would have silently lost verbose, tags and sources. One was added mirroring the existing catalogue entry exactly.

    Not done, and worth someone's attention separately: dpp4 also declares no DATASET_TYPE, so it would regenerate as regression while the catalogue records classification. That is a pre-existing bug unrelated to licensing and was left alone.

    5. The decision — taken 2026-08-19: option A

    The maintainer chose to comply: label the ChEMBL-derived datasets CC-BY-SA-3.0 and accept that the obligation propagates to downstream users. Recorded in ADR 009, which also documents the field shape and the three code paths that had to learn about it.

    Option C (ask EMBL-EBI) remains worth doing — it would settle whether ShareAlike legally attaches at all, and option A is the conservative answer taken without that confirmation. Doing so could only relax the position, never tighten it.

    The four positions as they were put, for the record:

    A. Comply — treat ChEMBL-derived datasets as Adaptations under CC-BY-SA-3.0. Label them CC-BY-SA-3.0, add ChEMBL attribution with release versions where known. Cost: downstream users of those eight datasets inherit a reciprocal obligation, and the catalogue becomes licence-heterogeneous. Benefit: unambiguously correct, and matches what ChEMBL asks for. Under this option esnuel_mca can proceed, with the caveat that its release version is unknown.

    B. Attribute but do not propagate — take the position that a ChemMatData dataset of computed properties keyed to structures is not an Adaptation of ChEMBL's selection, or that the underlying facts are unprotected. Add full attribution regardless. Cost: rests on a legal judgement the project would be making without counsel; should be written down as a deliberate position, not left implicit. Benefit: no obligation propagates to users; matches what the wider field does — DeepChem/MoleculeNet, TDC and OGB all redistribute ChEMBL-derived data under permissive licences.

    C. Ask EMBL-EBI. They have a licensing contact and answer this kind of question. Cost: time. Benefit: the answer is authoritative, settles all eight existing datasets and every future one, and is quotable in this file forever.

    D. Stop adding ChEMBL-derived datasets and decide separately what to do about the eight already published. Cost: excludes a large fraction of med-chem ML data. Probably only attractive if C comes back badly.

    What was recommended at the time: C first, then A or B depending on the answer — and do steps 1–3 of §4 immediately regardless, because they are required under every option and cost an afternoon. C is cheap, and the difference between A and B is precisely the thing EMBL-EBI is placed to answer and this project is not.

    Whatever is decided, record it in this file with the date and who decided, and add a line to rejected_datasets.md under "Checked and approved — do not re-litigate" so the extraction agent stops re-researching it.

    6. Not in question

    • ChEMBL permits redistribution. Nobody needs to establish that; the FAQ says so.
    • Commercial use is fine. SA is not NC.
    • The chem_mat_data package licence is unaffected. §4(b) does not reach the Collection, let alone the code.
    • The MCA/MAA values in esnuel_mca are not ChEMBL's. They are the authors' own r2SCAN-3c//GFN1-xTB computations. Only the smiles column is ChEMBL-derived. The same is true of most datasets in the table above — the structures are ChEMBL's, the measurements and computations are the papers'.

    7. How to tell whether a new dataset is ChEMBL-derived

    For the extraction agent, since this will keep coming up. ChEMBL provenance is usually one step removed and stated only in passing:

    • a CHEMBL\d+ identifier column, or target names like CHEMBL1862_Ki;
    • "drug-like subset", "bioactivity data", "curated from the literature" in a methods section, with ChEMBL named once in an acknowledgement rather than in the data statement — which is exactly how esnuel_mca states it;
    • benchmark lineage: MoleculeNet lipophilicity, MoleculeACE, the RDKit benchmarking platform, DUD-E actives and Briem–Lessel recreations are all ChEMBL underneath.

    bace_cls / bace_reg were checked and are not flagged as ChEMBL-derived by their own scripts; they are literature-compiled. That was verified rather than assumed, and is recorded here so it is not re-checked.
