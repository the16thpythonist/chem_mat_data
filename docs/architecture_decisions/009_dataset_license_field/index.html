    009 — Recording a licence on each dataset

    Status: accepted Date: 2026-08-19 Decided by: the maintainer

    Context

    ChemMatData redistributes other people's data, and until now carried no record of the terms it does so under. metadata.yml had thirteen fields — category, compounds, description, full, min_version, notes, raw, sources, tags, target_descriptions, target_type, targets, verbose — and none of them was a licence.

    That was tolerable while licences were established informally. It stopped being tolerable when the extraction pipeline (ADR 005) began systematically establishing a licence for every dataset it produces: stage 1 researches the terms, records them in the run manifest, and blocks on anything non-permissive until a human decides. The conclusion then went no further. A maintainer could accept a ShareAlike obligation on the project's behalf and the person who downloaded the dataset would never learn of it.

    The immediate trigger was ChEMBL. Ten datasets in the collection derive their structures from it, and ChEMBL is CC BY-SA 3.0 — attribution and ShareAlike. The attribution half was added on 2026-08-18. The ShareAlike half is an obligation that propagates to downstream users, and there was nowhere to put it. The full analysis is in ../chembl_licensing_analysis.md.

    Decision

    Add an optional license field to dataset metadata, shaped as a mapping:

    license:
    identifier: CC-BY-SA-3.0
    url: https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/
    note: >
        The molecular structures derive from ChEMBL, which is licensed CC BY-SA 3.0.
        The ShareAlike condition propagates, so this dataset is distributed under the
        same licence.

    A mapping rather than a bare SPDX string, for two reasons. First, provenance is usually the interesting part: "CC-BY-SA-3.0" alone does not say why, and the why is what a user needs in order to reason about their own obligations. Second, some datasets cannot be described by an identifier alone — esnuel_mca and esnuel_maa carry a ShareAlike obligation inherited from ChEMBL while their own upstream archive states no terms at all, and a bare identifier would assert a grant that was never made.

    The field is optional and its absence means "not established". Most of the collection predates any licence research. An absent license is not a claim that the data is freely usable, and nothing should read it that way.

    Applied now

    The twelve ChEMBL-derived datasets are labelled CC-BY-SA-3.0: dud_e, riniker_1, riniker_1_filtered, riniker_2, bl_chembl_reg, bl_chembl_cls, lipophilicity, dpp4, moleculeace, pparg_binding, esnuel_mca, esnuel_maa.

    The rest of the collection is untouched. Licences will be filled in as datasets are touched, and automatically for anything the extraction pipeline produces from now on.

    Consequences

    Users of the twelve inherit a reciprocal obligation. Someone building on lipophilicity or esnuel_mca must offer their derivative under CC-BY-SA-3.0 or a compatible licence. This is a real restriction the project is now placing on its users, deliberately, because it is the obligation ChEMBL attached to the data in the first place. Note it is not a restriction on commercial use — ChEMBL is explicit that ShareAlike does not preclude it.

    Scope is bounded by the licence text itself. CC BY-SA 3.0 §4(a) and §4(b) both end by stating that the obligation does not extend to "the Collection apart from the Work/Adaptation itself". The obligation attaches to the individual dataset. It does not reach the catalogue, the other datasets, or the chem_mat_data package.

    Three code paths had to learn about the field, all of which enumerate metadata keys explicitly and would otherwise have dropped it:

    • chem_mat_data/cli.py — cmdata info renders a License section, placed before Targets because whether a reader may use the dataset is more urgent than what is in it. Without this the label would exist only in a YAML file nobody reads.
    • chem_mat_data/manage.py — the field list in docs_compile_command.
    • chem_mat_data/agent/gates.py — the metadata gate warns when a draft carries no licence; the verification gate errors when a draft declares one and the shipped metadata.yml does not. The asymmetry is deliberate: forgetting to research a licence is a warning, but establishing one and failing to ship it defeats the entire point.

    What this does not settle. Whether ShareAlike legally attaches to a filtered, re-encoded dataset of computed properties is genuinely uncertain — facts are not copyrightable, the protected element would be ChEMBL's selection, and CC BY-SA 3.0 Unported does not mention database rights at all (verified: zero occurrences of "database" or "sui generis" in the legal code). This decision does not resolve that question; it takes the conservative side of it. Asking EMBL-EBI directly would settle it, and remains worth doing.

    Alternatives considered

    A bare license: CC-BY-SA-3.0 string. Simpler and easier to filter on, but cannot express inheritance or an unstated upstream without asserting something false.

    Attribute without propagating — credit ChEMBL, decline to pass the obligation on. This is what DeepChem/MoleculeNet, TDC and OGB effectively do. Rejected as a legal judgement the project would be making without counsel; the conservative option costs users a licence label, the permissive one costs them a defence.

    Licence every dataset now. Rejected as disproportionate: most have never had their licence researched, so it would mean either weeks of work or a great many honest "unknown" entries that add nothing.
