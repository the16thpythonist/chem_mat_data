    Rejected and restricted datasets

    Datasets that have been investigated and must not be added to ChemMatData, with the reason and the evidence behind it.

    This is the complement to MISSING_DATASETS.md, which lists candidates to add. The point of both is the same: not to spend an afternoon re-deriving a conclusion someone already reached.

    If you are the extraction agent: read this before stage 1. If the publication URL, the data record, or the upstream database you are about to investigate appears below, stop and tell the human, quoting the entry. Do not re-research it and do not proceed on your own judgement — the entries here were decided by a person, and only a person can change one.

    A rejection is not always permanent. Each entry records what would have to change.

    Rejected

    tmQMg-L — ligand library of transition metal complexes
    Publication Kneiding, H., Nova, A. & Balcells, D. Directional multiobjective optimization of metal complexes at the billion-system scale. Nat. Comput. Sci. 4, 263–273 (2024) — 10.1038/s43588-024-00616-5
    Data record https://github.com/uiocompcat/tmQMg-L · Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.10374523
    What it is 35,466 ligands cut out of the tmQMg complexes, with SMILES, metal-coordinating atom indices, NBO-derived formal charges and computed descriptors
    Decided 2026-08-13, by the maintainer
    Evidence rejected/tmqmg_l_charge-license-review.md

    Two independent reasons, either sufficient on its own.

    1. The data is CC BY-NC 4.0. The NonCommercial condition would bind every downstream ChemMatData user, not just this project. Verified rather than assumed: the LICENSE file is the unmodified CC BY-NC 4.0 legal code (MD5 fff4b145b044f5755732fda1d3aeda8c, first line Attribution-NonCommercial 4.0 International), zero occurrences of ShareAlike or NoDerivatives, and Zenodo's API independently reports cc-by-nc-4.0.

    Do not read GitHub's sidebar as evidence here. The GitHub API reports this repo as "spdx_id": "NOASSERTION", "name": "Other", which looks like an unclear licence and is not one — CC BY-NC 4.0 simply is not in GitHub's licence picklist. This misled the initial briefing for the run; the LICENSE file itself is unambiguous.

    2. The upstream is the Cambridge Structural Database, reached via tmQM and tmQMg. The CCDC's published position is that CSD data may not be redistributed, and its licence requires a separate signed agreement to distribute derived datasets externally. CSD refcodes appear verbatim in the redistributed files. No evidence of CCDC permission was found.

    Revisit if: the CCDC grants written permission for redistribution of CSD-derived data, or the authors relicense the record under permissive terms. Neither is likely without asking.

    Note: this was extracted once, on 2026-08-13, as a deliberate end-to-end test of the extraction pipeline, with the licence override approved for the test only. The dataset, its script and its 400 MB of artifacts were removed the same day. The licence review is kept because re-deriving the CCDC position took real work.

    polymer_tg — polymer glass transition temperatures
    Publication J. Chem. Inf. Model. 61 (2021) — 10.1021/acs.jcim.1c01031
    Data record The article's Supporting Information, files ci1c01031_si_002.xlsx and ci1c01031_si_003.xlsx
    What it is 566 polymers as repeat-unit SMILES with MD-simulated glass transition temperatures in °C
    Decided 2026-08-19, by the maintainer
    Status Script-only, never published

    The article reserves rights and the one permissive branch of its SI terms does not apply. pubs.acs.org is Cloudflare-walled, which is why an earlier pass could only argue from ACS's site-wide policy. A Wayback snapshot of the article record page (2025-06-22) renders the Supporting Information block in full, and it shows: "Copyright (c) 2021 American Chemical Society", a "Request reuse permissions" link, a hard paywall ("Purchase this article for 48 hours $48.00"), and zero occurrences of "Creative Commons" anywhere on the page. The SI terms read:

    "Such files may be downloaded by article for research use (if there is a public use license linked to the relevant article, that license may permit other uses). Permission may be obtained from ACS for other uses through requests via the RightsLink permission system."

    That permission is conditional and the condition fails — six independent indexes agree no public-use licence is linked to this article. So what exists is permission to download for research use, which is not permission to redistribute.

    A second, independent reason: the upstream is PoLyInfo (NIMS MatNavi), whose terms are registration-gated and prohibit bulk acquisition ("Web scraping of data is prohibited!").

    Revisit if: ACS grants a RightsLink permission for redistribution of ci1c01031_si_002.xlsx and ci1c01031_si_003.xlsx as a downloadable dataset — note ACS's permissions page pre-declares that it does not grant permission for some redistribution classes — or the authors republish the data under an open licence elsewhere. Do not re-research the licence itself: this was settled from the authoritative record, not inferred, and no further searching will change it.

    polymer_gas_permeability — polymer gas separation membranes
    Publication Sci. Adv. (2022) — 10.1126/sciadv.abn9545
    Data record https://github.com/jsunn-y/PolymerGasMembraneML
    What it is 778 polymers with experimentally measured gas permeabilities (log10 Barrer) for He, H2, O2, N2, CO2 and CH4
    Decided 2026-08-19, by the maintainer
    Status Script-only, never published

    A permissive wrapper licence contradicted by its own repository, over a database that forbids bulk acquisition. The repo carries a root LICENSE file containing the MIT licence, but:

    1. The repo's own README points to a CC-BY-NC (NonCommercial) paper, contradicting that MIT file.
    2. The upstream is PoLyInfo (NIMS MatNavi) — registration-gated, bulk acquisition prohibited — together with the Membrane Society of Australasia Polymer Gas Separation Membrane Database.

    This is deliberately treated differently from zinc250k, where an Apache-2.0 repository licence was accepted: there the data file sat inside the licensed repository with nothing contradicting it, and the upstream (ZINC) affirmatively permits sharing the results of a screen. Here the repository contradicts itself and the upstream prohibits the acquisition.

    Revisit if: the authors clarify which licence governs the data files, or NIMS grants redistribution permission for the PoLyInfo-derived values.

    price_small — Mcule purchasable-compound prices
    Publication none — this is a commercial vendor export, not a published dataset
    Data record Mcule "Purchasable (In Stock)" export (mcule_stock_210622_prices.csv), mcule.com Kft. (Budapest) / Mcule, Inc. (Palo Alto)
    What it is Mcule IDs, SMILES and per-tier USD prices for 8.49M compounds; the script keeps a ~1% (~85k-row) sample as the target price 4 (USD)
    Decided 2026-08-19, by the maintainer
    Status Script-only, never published; script now refuses to run

    No licence exists, and the vendor's terms affirmatively forbid the redistribution. This is the most thoroughly established negative in the licence review. Checked, over a decade of snapshots: the current and June-2021 Terms of Use (byte-identical), the database download page across 8 Wayback snapshots spanning 2015–2025, /legal (404), /privacy, the entire doc.mcule.com wiki, FAIRsharing and re3data. Zero licence, copyright or reuse statements anywhere. Mcule's Terms of Use then go further and prohibit what redistribution here would require.

    This is a commercial vendor's price list — the compound prices are the product Mcule sells. It is categorically unlike the research datasets elsewhere in this collection.

    Revisit if: Mcule grants written permission to redistribute a sample of the purchasable collection with prices. Nothing short of that changes the answer — do not re-research the licence, it was settled from the vendor's own terms across a decade of captures.

    tadf — TADF/OLED virtual screening molecules
    Publication Gómez-Bombarelli, R. et al. Design of efficient molecular organic light-emitting diodes by a high-throughput virtual screening and experimental approach. Nat. Mater. 15, 1120–1127 (2016) — 10.1038/nmat4717
    Data record None exists. The file used was source/tadf.csv on a KIT group file share
    What it is 460,199 organic molecules from a high-throughput virtual screen for thermally activated delayed fluorescence OLED emitters
    Decided 2026-08-19, by the maintainer
    Status Was published (460,199 compounds); removed from metadata.yml and the script now refuses to run. The files were left on the remote share.

    This is a provenance and consent failure, not a publisher-copyright one. An earlier pass blocked it on the Springer "all rights reserved" notice; that reasoning was wrong and has been corrected, though the conclusion stands.

    1. The Springer SI notice does not govern this data. The article's SI PDF contains roughly 300 structures (46 lead candidates, ~168 fragments, ~88 calibration compounds) — not 460,199. Decisively: all 46 SI lead-candidate SMILES were canonicalised and matched 46/46 against tadf.csv, and our file holds the full-precision values the SI only prints rounded — SI 0.121 / 0.061 / 2.94 / 0.07 against our 0.121228915 / 0.06096116 / 2.936687531 / 0.065375355. The SI is therefore a hand-rounded 0.01% extract of this file, not its source. Springer Nature also affirmatively disclaims intellectual property in "datasets connected to published papers", so the publisher is not a plausible rights-holder here at all.

    2. But nobody who might hold rights has granted anything. An exhaustive first-hand sweep — Zenodo, Figshare, Dataverse, OSF, GitHub, Hugging Face, Kaggle, DataCite under both corresponding-author names, PubChem, and the Matter Lab's own open-data page — found no open release of this data anywhere, ten years on. The nearest neighbours are all different data: the Clean Energy Project (Figshare 9640427, CC BY 4.0) is a photovoltaic screen with a different schema; Tartarus' gdb13.csv carries license: null; and the-matter-lab/Artificial-Design-of-Organic-Emitters claims Apache-2.0 in its README with no LICENSE file and is a different molecule set regardless.

    3. The authors' own most recent word on this exact dataset is access-gated. Friederich, Krenn, Tamblyn & Aspuru-Guzik, Mach. Learn.: Sci. Technol. 2, 025027 (2021) — itself published CC-BY-4.0, so these authors plainly know how to signal openness when they intend it — states the data is "available upon reasonable request from the authors". That is the opposite of an open release.

    4. Samsung co-authorship clouds who could even grant it. The original screen was a joint academic–industrial project with Samsung SAIT / Samsung Research America co-authors, so a willing academic author may lack unilateral authority to release it.

    5. The copy reached this project informally. Researcher-to-researcher, with no documented grant of redistribution rights.

    An open legal question is deliberately left open. Springer's site-terms restriction is contractual rather than copyright-based, which raises but does not settle whether a computed SMILES + TD-DFT property table is "facts" below the originality threshold (Feist) in the first place. Given the Ilm-NMR-P31 precedent in this same file — where EU sui generis database-right terms were taken seriously rather than assumed away — and ChemMatData's German context, that is a maintainer-level legal call, not one to settle in passing.

    Revisit if: the provenance can be established internally. The obvious lead is to ask Pascal Friederich (AiMat, KIT) how source/tadf.csv came to be on the group's bwSyncAndShare share, where it has sat since visual_graph_datasets' first release — he was a postdoc in the Aspuru-Guzik group, which is the natural explanation for a 460k-row file that exists nowhere public. If the answer is that the authors provided it, that is provenance no amount of web searching can recover, and the dataset may be revivable on that basis together with a permission from the authors. Failing that, a Springer Nature permission request would be needed.

    Partially restricted

    Ilm-NMR-P31 — ³¹P NMR chemical shifts
    Publication 10.1186/s13321-023-00792-y
    Data record https://zenodo.org/records/20762603 — record licence CC BY 4.0
    Decided 2026-08-12, by the maintainer
    Evidence dataset_redistribution_analysis.md

    The record's own licence is clean; the problem is upstream, and it splits the dataset in two:

    Source block Structures Position
    HBP — Tebby, Handbook of ³¹P NMR Data, CRC Press, 1991 6,608 Sui generis term expired 31 Dec 2012 (§137g(2) UrhG); US maker, likely outside Art. 11. Usable.
    Lan1/2/3 — Landolt-Börnstein III/40H, Springer, 2013/14 5,114 Term runs to ~2029; German maker. Not usable.

    Decision: the middle path — only the HBP-derived block may be used. The Landolt-Börnstein-derived structures are held back until the database right expires (~2029), unless Springer grants permission first.

    Second, independent reason not to publish this dataset at all as it stands: 196 of its geometries are a chemically different species from their own published formula — gas-phase optimisation let ion pairs react and waters of hydration dissociate, with one "bond" measuring 7.6 Å. That is a data-quality decision that has not been made, separate from the licence one.

    Already published, needs a decision

    tmqm and tmqmg are live in the collection and derive from the CSD through the same chain documented in the tmQMg-L entry above. The CCDC position recorded there applies to them by the same reasoning.

    This is deliberately not filed as a rejection: they are already distributed, and withdrawing a published dataset is a different decision from declining to add one. But it is the same question, and it is now written down rather than implicit. Their own record licences have not been examined in this project — that is the first thing to check if this is ever taken up.

    Checked and approved — do not re-litigate

    Not rejections. Recorded here so the same upstream question is not researched a third time.

    DataWarrior pKaInWater.dwar — the source behind pka_water and pka_acidic

    The article layer is clean (BMC applies the CC0 waiver to data in additional files). The values themselves come from the DataWarrior reference file, which carries no licence at all — and its author states on the project forum that the data was compiled from the literature and "the original paper reference got lost or was never collected", so no upstream licence can be established even in principle.

    Approved by the maintainer on 2026-08-13 with the reason recorded verbatim in .dataset_work/pka_acidic/manifest.yml. The decision covers the same Additional file 1 for both datasets.

    Settled — do not re-litigate

    ChEMBL and ShareAlike

    Decided 2026-08-19 by the maintainer. ChEMBL is CC BY-SA 3.0 — attribution and ShareAlike. The project complies: ChEMBL-derived datasets are attributed to ChEMBL and labelled CC-BY-SA-3.0 in their metadata, so the reciprocal obligation is passed on to whoever downloads them.

    Applies to twelve datasets: dud_e, riniker_1, riniker_1_filtered, riniker_2, bl_chembl_reg, bl_chembl_cls, lipophilicity, dpp4, moleculeace, pparg_binding, esnuel_mca, esnuel_maa.

    • Evidence and the options that were weighed: chembl_licensing_analysis.md
    • The field, its shape, and what it commits the project to: ADR 009

    If you are the extraction agent and your dataset is ChEMBL-derived: this no longer needs escalating on the ChEMBL ground alone. Record CC-BY-SA-3.0 in the licence stage with ChEMBL named as upstream_database, put the same identifier in the metadata draft's license field, and note that the gate will still escalate ShareAlike — that is expected, and the human override should cite this entry. Any other licence problem with your dataset is still yours to escalate on its own merits.

    Still worth doing, and would only relax this: asking EMBL-EBI whether ShareAlike legally attaches to a filtered, re-encoded dataset of computed properties. Option A was taken as the conservative answer without that confirmation.

    Adding an entry

    Record enough that nobody has to redo the research:

    • Every identifier the dataset can be reached by — publication DOI, record URL, mirrors. An agent arriving from a different link must still find the entry.
    • What was actually verified, not what was concluded. File hashes, quoted licence text, the URL of the terms. A conclusion with no evidence gets re-derived anyway.
    • Who decided and when. These are human decisions; an agent may not add or remove one.
    • What would have to change for the answer to be different.

    If the reasoning is long, put it in docs/rejected/ and link it, as with the tmQMg-L licence review.
